==============================================================================
COMPUTATION METHOD (how the K-divergence sweep is generated efficiently)
==============================================================================
Task: measure how action divergence grows with the number of sampled trajectories
K, sweeping K = 5,10,...,100 (step 5) then 100,150,...,1000 (step 50), from ONE
static observation. Script: generate_k_divergence_1000.py.

The naive way (regenerate K trajectories per K value, one trajectory at a time)
re-runs the whole policy ~21,000 times and re-encodes the same image/language
prefix every single time. Three changes make it ~12x cheaper WITHOUT changing
the sampled distribution:

  1. ENCODE THE VLM PREFIX ONCE (cached KV reuse).
     The observation (2 cameras + state + task text) is identical for every
     trajectory, so the heavy SigLIP/VLM forward is run a SINGLE time and its
     key/value cache is kept. Every trajectory then reuses that cache.
     -> VLM encodes once (~0.21 s) instead of ~2000 times.

  2. BATCH THE FLOW-MATCHING DENOISE (expand the cached KV to a mini-batch).
     Only the small action-expert denoise loop varies per trajectory. The
     batch-1 prefix KV cache is broadcast to a mini-batch of B=100 and B
     candidates are denoised in parallel (10 flow-matching steps), instead of
     looping one trajectory at a time.

  3. NESTED SUBSET (generate the max once, read every K off the front).
     Generate N_MAX = 1000 trajectories ONCE, then the divergence at each K is
     just the per-joint spread of the FIRST K of those 1000 (they are i.i.d., so
     the first K are a valid K-sample). One un-normalization covers all 1000.
     Note: this makes the K-curve monotonic by construction (adding samples can
     only widen max-min), which is why it is far smoother than independent runs.

Result (RTX 4060 Ti, smolvla): generating 1000 trajectories dropped from
67.5 s -> 14.2 s (14.2 ms/traj); VLM prefix encoded exactly once.

Divergence-vs-K fits a saturating exponential  y = A - B*exp(-K/tau):
   A (asymptote) = 30.76,  B = 8.91,  tau = 193.1,  R^2 = 0.987
Interpretation: divergence rises fast then plateaus toward ~30.8; ~63% of the
ceiling is reached by K~=193, and it is essentially saturated past K~=600.

Full per-K blocks: Research_notes_divergence_1000.txt
Machine-readable:  divergence_summary_1000.csv   Plot: divergence_vs_k_1000.png
Caveat: the averaged y mixes units (5 arm joints in degrees + gripper closure).


==============================================================================
FINDING: pi0.5 diverges WIDER than SmolVLA (bigger model -> more spread)
==============================================================================
Both policies run with the IDENTICAL observation, noise scheme, nested-subset
method, dense-at-the-bend K sampling (K=2..1000), log-K plot, and saturating fit.
Scripts: generate_k_divergence_1000.py (SmolVLA), generate_k_divergence_pi05.py (pi0.5).

Saturating-exponential fit  y = A - B*exp(-K/tau)   (both: dense K to 1000, log-K):
   SmolVLA (~0.5B):  A = 30.33,  B = 9.83,  tau = 125.0,  R^2 = 0.894
   pi0.5   (~4B):    A = 38.72,  B = 15.00, tau = 125.0,  R^2 = 0.937

Result: pi0.5 is WIDER. Its divergence ceiling (asymptote A) is ~38.7 vs ~30.3
for SmolVLA -- about +28% more trajectory spread at saturation under the same
observation. The larger / better model explores a noticeably larger action
manifold from a single observation.

Notably, with the SAME methodology both models have the SAME knee timescale
tau ~= 125 -- i.e. both saturate at about the same sample count; the models differ
in the HEIGHT of the ceiling (spread width), NOT in how fast they get there. This
matters for PVD: pi0.5's wider candidate set gives the physics-verified selector
MORE distinct candidates to choose among per step, for the same K.

Notes: R^2 (~0.89-0.94) is held down by the very-low-K points (K=2..4), which are
high-variance single draws; on log-K the data read slightly sigmoidal.
Same mixed-units caveat on the averaged y (5 joints deg + gripper closure).

SmolVLA: Research_notes_divergence_1000.txt / divergence_summary_1000.csv / divergence_vs_k_1000.png
pi0.5:   Research_notes_divergence_pi05.txt / divergence_summary_pi05.csv / divergence_vs_k_pi05.png


==============================================================================
VALIDATION: S_vel is a grounded FEASIBILITY term (tested on real hardware)
==============================================================================
Claim: the velocity term S_vel = Sigma max(0, |q_dot| - q_dot_max) corresponds to
a REAL, measurable failure of the SO-101 arm -- not just a number.

Ground truth. We use tracking error e_j(t) = q_cmd,j(t) - q_ach,j(t+L) as the
physical "infeasible" signal: if the arm cannot follow a command, the commanded
joint position and the achieved joint position diverge. This needs only position
reads (the torque route failed earlier -- measured servo load is friction-
dominated, r~0.26 vs velocity r~0.8, so it cannot ground the scorer).

Data. One SmolVLA rollout on the physical SO-101 (qualia smolvla checkpoint),
852 steps at ~30 Hz, recorded with record_rollout.py, which logs -- per control
tick, time-synced -- the policy command (cmd_, what Phi scores), the safety-capped
command actually sent (sent_, after --robot.max_relative_target=10), and the
achieved position (pos_, Present_Position). File: pvd_logs/record_run1.csv.
The achieved position lags the command by L=4 ticks (~132 ms), found by minimising
mean |e|; all correlations use that alignment.

Test + result. Per joint, correlate |commanded velocity| against |tracking error|:
   mean body-joint r = +0.362.
A higher commanded speed co-occurs with larger tracking error, on EVERY body joint.

Ruling out the safety cap (the key control). Because --robot.max_relative_target
clips large commands, part of that correlation could be the CAP, not the servo.
We re-ran with every cap-affected tick masked out (any clip in the alignment
window [t, t+L]). The correlation SURVIVES:
   mean body-joint r = +0.269 (cap-masked).
Per joint (cap-masked r): shoulder_pan 0.436, wrist_roll 0.359, wrist_flex 0.264,
elbow_flex 0.232, shoulder_lift 0.054. shoulder_pan and wrist_flex are IDENTICAL
masked vs unmasked (they were never capped) -- clean servo evidence.

VERDICT: S_vel VALIDATED as a feasibility term. Commanding a joint faster than it
can move produces measurable tracking failure, and the effect is grounded in servo
behaviour (survives cap-masking), not an artefact of the safety cap. It stays in
the feasibility block of Phi.

Scope / caveats (state these in the paper):
  * Moderate, not strong (r~0.27). Velocity explains PART of tracking failure;
    torque/gravity explain the rest -- expected, and it is why S_vel coexists with
    the torque term rather than replacing it.
  * The THRESHOLD q_dot_max is currently wrong. The scorer uses a placeholder
    q_dot_max = 3 rad/s (172 deg/s), but the policy commands up to ~20 rad/s and
    the cap (5.2 rad/s) is what actually binds. A feasibility penalty with a
    mis-set limit fires at the wrong point. q_dot_max MUST be measured per joint
    (single-joint ramp test) before S_vel is quantitatively useful. The term is
    validated; its limit is not yet calibrated.
  * Division of labour on the gravity joint. On shoulder_lift (heaviest, capped
    most) the velocity<->error link collapses to r=0.05 once cap ticks are removed
    -- there the real limiter is torque/gravity, which belongs to the TORQUE term,
    not S_vel. So: S_vel owns velocity-limited joints; the torque term owns
    gravity-limited ones.
  * Single trajectory -- r values are indicative; a multi-trajectory Phi-vs-total-
    error sweep is the next grounding step.

Contrast (companion results, same data/method):
  * S_acc failed as a FEASIBILITY term (velocity-controlled partial r vs tracking
    error ~= +0.01) but is grounded as a SMOOTHNESS term (partial r vs measured
    jerk = +0.16, up to +0.41 on the low-inertia wrist joint) -- it belongs with
    the torque/smoothness block, not the limit block.
  * S_cont is not grounded in rollout data (boundary-proximity partial r ~= -0.05
    over n=4035; the policy conditions each chunk on q0 and the cap smooths jumps)
    -- needs a dedicated jump test.

Scripts: record_rollout.py (recording), validate_kinematic_terms.py +
validate_reanalysis.py (S_vel/S_acc/S_cont), validate_sacc_smoothness.py.
Plot: kinematic_terms_validation.png.
